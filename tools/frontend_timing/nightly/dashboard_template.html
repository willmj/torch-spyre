Does time move with the work?

Both indexed to the first measured night, which is the only honest way to share an axis between a duration and a count. Lines that move together mean the work itself changed; lines that diverge mean the same work ran at a different speed, which is the machine and not the compiler.

Share taken by the hot pass

Scratchpad planning as a percentage of frontend time, inclusive of its substages. This is the one series measured inclusive rather than self: adding a timer inside the pass moves time into a child and would shrink a self-time line without anything getting faster.

Over time

Where the hot pass spends it

Its substages, each as a percentage of frontend time. A drop in one that reappears in another is a reattribution, not a saving -- which is exactly what happened when these timers were added. A gap means the night predates that timer.

Over time

Work counters per operation

On their own chart because they are counts, not milliseconds, and a second y-scale is the one chart mistake worth refusing. These are the signal to watch: across a 50-point sweep the counters varied 0.00% between samples against 3.68% for times, so a step here is a real change and a timing wobble usually is not.
